# Plan an essay

## Workflow

1. Read the brief, marking criteria, relevant course materials and latest agreed plan or feedback.
2. Always interview the user before creating or substantively revising the plan. Use the question tool when available. Establish the intended angle, content priorities, depth and voice; reuse settled answers from the current conversation.
3. Present a short passage of essay prose on the assignment topic. Refine its reasoning and wording until the user explicitly agrees. Agreement already reached for this assignment counts. Do not write the plan while awaiting their answer.
4. Create `PLAN.md` in the project root, or update the named planning file. Preserve agreed decisions. Draft the essay only if separately requested.

## Personal preferences

- Use a natural second-year student voice: simple words, short connected sentences and intuitive explanations, without an expert or textbook tone.
- Ground reasoning in lecture concepts and assigned readings. Show how they explain the evidence or support the argument; citations alone do not establish that connection. Use external theory only where the assignment needs it.
- Keep required analysis, but omit optional sophistication and repeated caveats. Explain necessary specialist terms briefly.
- Where maths helps, use a short explanation before the equation, then a brief interpretation. Do not impose equations or a lab-report structure on other subjects.

## PLAN.md content

Use the assignment's structure and enough specific guidance for another writer to proceed without this conversation:

- Question, intended answer or provisional thesis, scope and agreed emphasis.
- Agreed writing approach and approved prose sample, including boundaries on depth and content.
- Section or argument outlines specifying what to establish, which course concepts and evidence to use, and how they support the answer. Replace vague directions such as "discuss critically" with the actual issue to examine.
- Sources beside the relevant content, with verified locators where needed. Flag missing support and unresolved decisions with next steps.
- Flexible space allocations where useful, and planned visuals only where relevant.
- A short, unchecked checklist tied to the criteria and agreed style.
