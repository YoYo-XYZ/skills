# Physics lab writing style

Focus on how the student understands and explains the experiment. This skill guides voice, emphasis and reasoning, rather than report structure or presentation.

## Voice

- Write like a second-year student explaining what they understand. Use familiar words, straightforward grammar and short, connected sentences. Avoid the tone of an expert research paper or textbook.
- Be natural and direct. Remove polished filler, unnecessary jargon and complicated phrasing. Do not deliberately introduce mistakes to sound like a student.

## Reasoning and emphasis

- Start from relevant lecture ideas and use them to explain what happened in the experiment. Make the connection through the physical relationship, not just a citation or concept name.
- Explain what a change in the experiment should do, then compare that expectation with the measured result. Keep the reasoning close to the actual experiment.
- Distinguish observations, model predictions and assumptions. Explain meaningful differences without forcing agreement or listing every possible error. Keep limitations proportional to their effect on the interpretation.

## Maths and concision

- Use equations to express relationships that would take too many words. Give a short explanation of the physical idea before the maths, then interpret only what the reader needs. Avoid repeating the same relationship in several forms.
- Include enough explanation to make the reasoning easy to follow. Prefer a few clear sentences over long verbal descriptions of physics. Keep necessary detail; leave out optional sophistication, repetition and analysis that does not help answer the assignment.
